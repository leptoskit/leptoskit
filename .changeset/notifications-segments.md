Person attributes, segments, and send to everyone. The service keeps a registry of the people it knows — registered when the app's server sets their attributes, when they subscribe to a topic, set a category preference, or enroll a push endpoint, and backfilled from those tables by the migration; a person named only in a recipients send is never registered. The app's server describes people with `set_person_attributes` (batch, ≤1,000 people, idempotent; keys `a-z0-9_` ≤64 bytes, values ≤256 bytes, ≤50 attributes per person with cap-skipping counted; `person_attributes` reads back; `forget_person` removes the person from the registry with their attributes and topic subscriptions, keeping their inbox) — app-only by design, so a segment can't be forged from the client side. The condition grammar extends FCM's with `user.<key> == 'v'`, `!=`, `in ['a','b']`, `has(user.<key>)`, and finite-number `<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=` (true only when both sides parse as finite numbers; `NaN`/`inf` literals refused), mixing freely with `'t' in topics` under `&&`/`||`/`!`/parens with byte-offset errors and bounds (≤5 topics, ≤10 attribute predicates, depth ≤8, ≤1 KiB); an absent attribute answers false to every predicate, and the everyone-in-disguise refusal now checks topics _and_ attributes. `SendTarget::Everyone` reaches every registered person, and a condition no longer needs to be anchored on a topic: one that structurally requires a topic still fans out over the topic index, while an attribute-anchored one (and everyone) fans out over the registry — same batched, cursor-advancing, idempotent fanout job, same opt-out/visibility/push/live behaviour, `validate_only` counting every target. SQLite and Postgres migrations add `persons` and `person_attributes` with the registry backfill.

Audience partitioning by topics and conditions, with a durable batched fanout. A send can now target `SendTarget::Topic` or `SendTarget::Condition` beside today's `SendTarget::Recipients`: the app's server subscribes people to per-person topics in idempotent batches (`subscribe_to_topic`/`unsubscribe_from_topic`, at most 1,000 people per call; a person holds at most 500 topics, and a full person is skipped and counted rather than refusing the batch), and a person may subscribe themselves (`subscribe`, `unsubscribe`, `my_topics`, live and paginated) to the topics the operator opened in `LEPTOSKIT_NOTIFICATIONS_OPEN_TOPICS` — patterns like `news.*`, nothing open by default, and the person always read from the forwarded identity. Conditions use FCM's grammar verbatim (`'a' in topics && ('b' in topics || !('c' in topics))`), validated on both sides with byte-offset errors, FCM's bounds (≤5 topics, depth ≤8, ≤1 KiB), and unanchored conditions — ones true for a person holding no topics — refused, since there is no everyone audience. A topic or condition send answers `fanning_out` immediately and a job walks the audience in 500-person batches — one transaction per batch for the inbox rows, push deliveries, and the stored cursor, unique per send and person so a crashed or replayed batch never duplicates, the audience snapshotted as the fanout reaches each person. `send_status` answers the state and counts; `validate_only` counts the audience without writing; `cancel` stops a fanout mid-walk and cancels the rows written so far. SQLite and Postgres migrations add the `topic_subscriptions` table, the fanout bookkeeping on `sends`, and the per-send-per-person uniqueness on `notifications` (existing rows backfilled). Breaking: `Send` and `check_send` take the target — `recipients: vec![…]` becomes `target: SendTarget::Recipients(vec![…])` — and `SendResult` gains `state` and `targeted`.

Browser push enrollment, corrected to the browser's real lifecycle: the client half of Web Push. A new `browser` feature ships the receiver service-worker script (built with your inbox route, served same-origin at the root), a typed availability check, and `enroll`, `reconcile`, and `disable` flows that ride the person's own RPC identity over the feature's own same-origin fetch transport — no service token, nothing persisted in the worker. `reconcile` runs on launch and registers the subscription the browser already holds without ever prompting, answering `None` when there is none; `enroll` runs from the gesture that can subscribe. Enrollment waits for the selected worker's own activation (a failed install errors instead of hanging), matches a requested scope exactly rather than letting a broader worker stand in, and reuses an existing subscription only when it was created for your own VAPID key — another sender's is refused, not replaced. The enrollment handle records the subscription and scope it owns, so `disable` revokes first and then unsubscribes exactly that subscription: a stale handle leaves a replaced subscription and another scope's enrollment alone, and a retried disable whose revoke already landed completes. The receiver shows the service's envelope under a tag built from the notification's id, falls back to one truthful generic banner for missing, oversized, malformed, or mistyped pushes, and routes clicks only to your own inbox route on your own origin.

## [0.3.1](https://github.com/leptoskit/leptoskit/releases/tag/v0.3.1) (2026-11-28)

### Features

- **Textured models packed by gltfpack.** Scenes read `KHR_texture_transform`, so a textured model compressed by `gltfpack` draws its textures where they belong. gltfpack quantizes texture coordinates and records the scale and offset that undo it as a texture transform; scenes used to ignore it, and every texture was sampled from its corner. The transform is applied to a primitive's texture coordinates as the model loads. A material's textures share one transform, the first found starting from the base color texture, which is what gltfpack writes. _Owner:_ Ifiok Jr. · _Introduced in:_ [fa18362](https://github.com/leptoskit/leptoskit/commit/fa18362be9c37b7ccfb201dc4a1d103541c498ab)
- **Deliver primary-pointer drags, taps and cancellations from Android, iOS and macOS scene views through `Scene::next_pointer`.** Movement uses layout units and a bounded queue coalesces drags independently of model-loading events. _Owner:_ Ifiok Jr. · _Introduced in:_ [7237130](https://github.com/leptoskit/leptoskit/commit/723713098b125431f6e762cbf4b6f3ebe5506a23)
- **Additive light for glows, flashes, and sparks.** `Scene::set_blend(node, part, Blend::Additive)` (`Op::SetBlend`) makes a part of a model add its colour to what is drawn behind it, scaled by its opacity, instead of covering it. A muzzle flash, a spark, or a soft halo then reads as light over a camera picture, and black adds nothing, so a round glow needs no cut-out. `Blend::Normal` puts it back. Additive parts draw with the transparent parts. The op is new, so batches without it encode exactly as before. _Owner:_ Ifiok Jr. · _Introduced in:_ [284fd88](https://github.com/leptoskit/leptoskit/commit/284fd8831fbc6ae5e085c4270152e74863c2965c)
- **A hand-drawn style: banded light and ink lines.** `Scene::set_style` (`Op::SetStyle`) draws a scene like an inked drawing. `Shading::Toon` lights surfaces in 2 to 4 flat bands with antialiased edges, a coloured shade for the darkest band, flat ambient light, a hard highlight and a hard rim, and optional hatching fixed to the surface. `Ink` draws a line around every closed opaque model as an inverted hull in the same pass (alpha-masked cutouts, blended, faded, and additive parts skip the hull while keeping inner lines and hatching): its width is thousandths of the view's height, so it holds on every screen and in each eye, and it can thicken on the shadow side, wobble like a pen, and boil in steps on the scene's clock without asking for frames. Outline normals are welded at load, so hard edges stay closed, and the line follows skins, morph targets, and animation. `Scene::set_ink` (`Op::SetInk`) weighs, colours, or drops a part's line (weights below 1 taper the outline's 1-pixel floor smoothly to 0 so a line can fade out before fading the part), and a model can weigh its own per vertex in `COLOR_1`'s red channel. `Style::default()` is the physically based look, and scenes draw byte for byte as before without a style. The ops (`SetStyle` tag 23, `SetInk` tag 24) are additive `VERSION = 1` ops: batches without them encode identically and stay compatible with older v1 hosts, while batches containing them require a matching host/renderer version (older v1 hosts reject the batch as an unknown op). _Owner:_ Ifiok Jr. · _Introduced in:_ [596a217](https://github.com/leptoskit/leptoskit/commit/596a21736433d42e615466ece1de80ecf373b01f)
- **Eased transforms and crossfades, and clips from a phase.** `Scene::ease_transform` (`Op::EaseTransform`) moves a node from wherever it is to a transform over a number of seconds along an `Easing` (`Linear`, `Smooth` for smoothstep, `In`, or `Out`), asking for frames until it arrives and landing on the exact transform sent; a `set_transform` or another ease takes over from where it has got to. `Play::fade_easing` eases a crossfade instead of blending at a steady rate, and `Play::phase` starts a clip part of the way through, so instances playing one clip need not move in step. Linear crossfades from the start of a clip stay the default. The ops (`EaseTransform` tag 25, eased/phased `Play` tag 26) are additive `VERSION = 1` ops: batches without them encode identically and stay compatible with older v1 hosts, while batches containing them require a matching host/renderer version (older v1 hosts reject the batch as an unknown op). _Owner:_ Ifiok Jr. · _Introduced in:_ [596a217](https://github.com/leptoskit/leptoskit/commit/596a21736433d42e615466ece1de80ecf373b01f)

#### Move a model's own parts from your app

_Owner:_ Ifiok Jr. · _Introduced in:_ [107f77a](https://github.com/leptoskit/leptoskit/commit/107f77af379ab2edaa493f6f6de56d4180cc84c4)

`Scene::set_part_offset(node, part, offset, seconds)` (`Op::SetPartOffset`) moves a named part of a model instance, a bone or any node, from where its rest pose or the playing clip puts it. The offset turns the part about its own origin and moves it along its own axes, and everything under the part follows, skinned meshes included, so an app can turn a creature's head towards the camera while its walk clip plays, swing a rigid leg, or open a hinged lid by exactly as much as its own state says.

The host moves the part there over `seconds`, in a straight line from where it is now, on its own display clock. An app that works out a pose on each tick of its own clock and sends it with the tick's length is drawn smoothly between ticks. `Transform::IDENTITY` puts the part back. The op is new, so batches without it encode exactly as before.

- **Relief-print shading, paper knockout, and camera lights.** `Shading::Print(Print)` and `Style::print()` add a three-band un-tonemapped relief-print shading mode to `Op::SetStyle`, paired with per-target `Op::SetPrint` (`Scene::set_print`) overrides (`Part` > `Material`, exact name over base name > `All`). Relief-print shading skips PBR textures (preserving their alpha for `AlphaMode::Mask` cutouts and `AlphaMode::Blend`) and tonemapping, draws symmetric `fwidth`-antialiased object-space hatching and cross-hatching that fade to their exact mean tone as pitch shrinks, adds turning-edge paper specular and backlit spot rim highlights, modulates key ink width with a smoothstep light-side curve, gives each part a deterministic IEEE CRC-32 wobble seed with per-figure 3-slot boil, and renders a single-pass stencil paper knockout (`Depth24PlusStencil8`) with per-figure stencil references so overlapping figures separate while models joined with `Op::Attach` have no interior seam and open single-sided sheets frame cleanly. Styles without a knockout stay on `Depth32Float` and draw byte-for-byte unchanged. `Light::camera_space` rotates the directional light per view, and `Asset::from_slice_with_options` (`LoadOptions::outline_smooth_rings` / glTF `extras.outlineSmoothRings`) optionally smooths welded outline normals across up to 8 vertex rings. _Owner:_ Ifiok Jr. · _Introduced in:_ [4653fcf](https://github.com/leptoskit/leptoskit/commit/4653fcfdc2d617e2201aa1cecf59b02367b60ea9)

#### 3D scenes over the live camera on iOS and Android

_Owner:_ Ifiok Jr. · _Introduced in:_ [c215d24](https://github.com/leptoskit/leptoskit/commit/c215d24d84051613fef7bf2171094a72208dabce)

A `Scene3d` whose backdrop is `Backdrop::Camera` now draws over the camera while an `xr` session runs on a phone. The generated iOS and Android apps hand the scene the session's newest frame: the camera picture behind the models, turned upright for the screen's rotation and cropped to fill the view, the session's own viewpoint in place of the scene's camera, and the room's measured depth hiding the parts of the scene behind real things. Start the session with `Features::CAMERA_IMAGE` (and `Features::DEPTH` for occlusion). Without a session the scene draws as before, transparent behind its models.

Android phones without ARCore, such as the Solana Seeker, now show the back camera in `Orientation` sessions too, through Camera2, with the rotation sensor turning the view and the camera's own field of view keeping the scene to the picture's scale. Such a session leaves the picture out, rather than failing, until the user allows the camera, unless it requires `CAMERA_IMAGE`.

On Android a `Scene3d` showing the camera moves below the window's own views, so the interface laid over it stays visible.

For hosts that draw scenes themselves, `leptoskit_3d::host::Hub::set_camera_feed` takes the picture a camera backdrop draws over, `View::set_rotation` the interface's rotation, and the bridge adds `lk_scene3d_view_set_rotation` and `lk_scene3d_view_shows_camera`. `leptoskit_xr`'s new `scene3d` feature makes the plugin's sessions that feed, and `leptoskit build` turns it on, with any other async plugin's `scene3d` feature, when the app shows 3D scenes. `leptoskit_xr::host::install_depth_estimator` (or `XrRuntime::set_depth_estimator`) takes a `DepthEstimator`, which turns small CPU copies of the camera picture (`CameraFrame`, with the camera's `Intrinsics`) into depth for phones that measure none.

#### Immersive sessions from Meta Quest panel apps

_Owner:_ Ifiok Jr. · _Introduced in:_ [e897f31](https://github.com/leptoskit/leptoskit/commit/e897f31c27dbc9cb0c39378f56f705e9ef8e83f8)

A Quest app now draws its 3D scene into the headset. Start an `ImmersiveVr` or `ImmersiveAr` session and choose the scene with the new `Session::present(Some(scene_id))`: each eye sees the scene from the player's eye, over passthrough in `ImmersiveAr`. An app built with `[native.quest] immersive = false`, a 2D panel, opens an immersive activity over the panel for the session and comes back to the panel in Horizon Home when the session ends, while its code keeps running and receiving frames.

`leptoskit build --platform quest` declares that activity in every Quest app, marks a panel app's launcher with Horizon OS's 2D category and targets Horizon OS v69, and turns on `leptoskit_xr`'s new `quest` feature (or any async plugin's `quest` feature). For hosts that draw scenes themselves, `leptoskit_3d::host::Hub::draw_scene` draws a scene on another device, such as a headset's, and `Hub::forget_device` moves scenes off it again.

None of this has run on a headset yet.

### Fixes

- **Transparent scenes show the page behind them in the browser.** A scene with `Backdrop::Transparent` drew over solid black in the browser. wgpu lists only `Opaque` as a WebGPU canvas's alpha mode, though every browser canvas also takes `premultiplied`, so the canvas was set up opaque. Browser canvases now composite premultiplied, as native surfaces already did. A 3D view over a photo, a camera picture, or another 3D view shows them through its empty pixels. WebGL2, where wgpu refuses anything but opaque, is unchanged. _Owner:_ Ifiok Jr. · _Introduced in:_ [33ad5e1](https://github.com/leptoskit/leptoskit/commit/33ad5e1171f97f4086bf6ec9290e75aef956cdc0)

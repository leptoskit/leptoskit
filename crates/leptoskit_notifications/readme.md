# leptoskit_notifications

Durable notifications for LeptosKit apps: a first-party service, the send format its app's server uses, and the inbox its clients read.

An app's server sends to explicit user ids, to a topic's subscribers, or to the people a condition matches — FCM's grammar, `'TopicA' in topics && ('TopicB' in topics || !('TopicC' in topics))` — and the service stores one durable inbox row per recipient, in a database of its own, with idempotency by send key: the same key with the same intent answers the first run's result across restarts — whenever it is asked, expiry included — and the same key with a different intent is a conflict. Recipients read their own inbox from any device their app signs in on — the web, a native app, or the server while it renders — paginated and bounded, with per-person read, dismissed, and cancelled state shared across devices. Categories and opt-out preferences are enforced at send time and at visibility, and changing one reaches screens that are already open. Scheduled sends become visible when their time comes and can be cancelled until then; expired rows leave every view. Open screens follow all of it live: the service runs wake jobs at each send's turning points, so what time changes, the watching screen re-reads.

Topics are per person — every device signed in as the person receives what the person receives. The app's server subscribes people in batches (`subscribe_to_topic`, ≤1,000 per call, idempotent, at most 500 topics held), and a person may subscribe themselves (`subscribe`, `unsubscribe`, `my_topics`) to the topics the operator opened in `LEPTOSKIT_NOTIFICATIONS_OPEN_TOPICS`, patterns like `news.*`; nothing is open by default. A topic, condition, or everyone send can address any number of people: `send` records the send and enqueues a fanout job, answers `fanning_out`, and the job walks the audience in batches of 500 — one transaction per batch for the rows, the push deliveries, and the stored cursor, idempotent per send and person — while `send_status` reports the progress and `validate_only` counts the audience without writing. A cancel stops a fanout mid-walk.

Segments extend conditions from topics to people: the app's server describes people with attributes (`set_person_attributes`, ≤1,000 people and ≤50 attributes each, keys `a-z0-9_` up to 64 bytes, values up to 256 bytes, idempotent; `person_attributes` reads back, `forget_person` erases everything the service holds about a person — attributes, subscriptions, preferences, devices, and inbox), and the condition grammar gains `user.<key> == 'v'`, `!=`, `in [...]`, `has(user.<key>)`, and finite-number comparisons `<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=`, mixing freely with `'t' in topics` under `&&`, `||`, `!`, and parentheses (≤5 topics, ≤10 attribute predicates, depth ≤8, ≤1 KiB; a value may hold spaces, and a quote written twice, `'O''Brien'`). Attributes are app-only: a person can't set or read them, so segments are never forgeable. Which index a condition's fanout walks follows from its anchor — a topic-anchored condition walks the topic's subscribers, an attribute- or device-anchored one the person registry.

Devices are targetable too. Every push enrollment carries what the device says about itself — its locale (a BCP 47 tag, stored in canonical case), its IANA time zone (checked for shape), and the app's version — sent by the web, iOS, Android, and macOS clients with no app code and replaced on re-enrollment without rotating the credential; only the endpoint's owner reads them back. Conditions gain `device.platform`, `device.locale`, `device.language`, `device.app_version` (compared as versions: numeric dot-separated parts, missing parts 0, anything from `-` or `+` ignored), and `device.time_zone`, with the `user.` operators, and an unknown key is refused by name. A device predicate is judged per device: a person is in the audience when the condition holds with no device or on one of their live endpoints, gets one inbox row, and the push goes only to the endpoints it holds for — decided where the deliveries are written, from one endpoint read per batch. The registry is who "everyone" means: `SendTarget::Everyone` reaches every person the service knows — registered by an attribute, a topic subscription, a category preference, or a push enrollment, and backfilled from those tables by the migration — with category opt-outs skipped as everywhere.

A send can be written once for many languages and time zones. `SendSpec::variants` carries its text per BCP 47 tag, and each person's one inbox row and its push are written in the variant their locale matches (the exact tag, then shorter, down to the language, then the send's own text). `SendSpec::local_time` names a wall-clock time instead of an instant, and each person's row becomes visible, and is pushed, when that time arrives in their IANA time zone. Language and zone resolve per person: the `locale` or `time_zone` attribute the app set, then their most recently seen live endpoint, then the send's default.

When the operator configures a push transport — APNs by `.p8` key, FCM by service account, or Web Push by VAPID pair, each from the service's environment — the same send also reaches the devices its recipients enrolled: one durable delivery per notification and endpoint, attempted by a background job at the notification's visibility, retried on transient failures with the provider's `Retry-After` honored, ended clearly on permanent ones, and reported to the app as sanitized per-endpoint status. Delivery is at-least-once, collapse-tagged by the notification's id, and provider acceptance is as far as the service sees — never a person's receipt. Enrollment is the signed-in person's own act through a typed credential; the credential never appears in a list, a status row, or an error, and only its owner revokes it.

Every send can be shaped with `MessageOptions`: a silent data message (`kind: "data"` — no banner anywhere, no inbox row, no badge count, durable delivery all the same), priority, an image, a sound, a badge (`Unread` resolves per recipient at push time to their live unread count), a click link, and the per-platform `android`, `apple`, and `web` overrides — each carried where its platform reads it, validated on both sides, counted in the send's idempotent intent, and dropped in a documented order when a provider's payload limit says so. A send's `android.channel_id` is the id the app gave the local-notifications plugin's `configure_channel`: the FCM transport sends it under the plugin's `leptoskit.notifications.` prefix, which is how the channel is named on the device, and a push that names no channel uses the app's `default` one. `send_test` (app-only) pushes a spec to one person's live endpoints once each — FCM's "test on device" — answering per endpoint and writing nothing.

What this crate does and does not cover on the enrollment's client half: the **web half is here** — the `browser` module (behind the feature of the same name) ships the receiver service-worker script, enrolls the browser's subscription over the person's own RPC identity, and disables it again — and so are the **native halves** — the `native` module turns the device token the generated app forwarded into the same enrollment, an APNs device token on iOS and macOS and an FCM registration token on Android, reconciles a previously opted-in device at launch without prompting (rotated tokens included, old handles retired under their owner; an enrollment the server revoked is reported gone and never re-created), and disables it again, with the same boundaries. The plugin contract it rides on is an optional dependency the `native` feature turns on for the guest alone, so the shared types and the service build without it. The receiver worker claims the pages already open when it activates, so a click can route the tab a person enrolled from, and a click on an action button adds the button's id to the opened address as `lka`. There is no delivery receipt: the service knows what the provider accepted and nothing more. Device details are what each client reports — no OS version or model, and `apns` covers iOS and macOS alike. Compose received items with the local-notifications plugin (`leptoskit_plugin_notifications`) where the platform supports it, and see the notifications guide for the platform gaps.

The service deletes what is over, once an hour, in small batches: inbox rows a year after they became visible, expired and cancelled rows and finished push deliveries after 30 days, and a send's record — its idempotency key — after 90 days and only once no row of the send remains. Each is a number of days the operator sets (`LEPTOSKIT_NOTIFICATIONS_RETENTION_INBOX_DAYS`, `_ENDED_DAYS`, `_DELIVERIES_DAYS`, `_SENDS_DAYS`), or `off`; nothing a live send, a queued delivery, or a scheduled notification still needs is deleted. When embedding, `service::workers_with_settings` takes the `service::config::Retention` to use.

Add the service with `[services.notifications]` in `leptoskit.toml`; see the notifications guide on the site for the whole flow, the transport variables, the open-topic patterns, and what push costs you.

## Worker runtime ownership

When embedding the push service, pass clones of the same `service::push::Push` configuration to `service::routes_with_push` and `service::workers_with_push` — or both that and the open-topic patterns to `service::routes_with_settings`. Each database's workers own their transports. Rebuild the runtime on restart; pending jobs use that fresh runtime and persist only send and round IDs. `service::workers` and `service::routes` remain usable without push configured.

Delivery retries stop at eight actual push requests, including requests refused for authentication. OAuth token exchanges are separate and do not spend push attempts. FCM refreshes once after a 401; the next push goes through the durable queue and its attempt budget. An exchange failure retries every minute with sanitized `operator_configuration` status. Neither provider diagnostics nor credentials appear in that status.

Every delivery has a deadline: the earlier of notification expiry and 24 hours after `visible_at`, including notifications with no expiry. Provider `Retry-After` accepts delta seconds and HTTP dates; invalid values use backoff. A provider delay is never shortened into an earlier push. If it exceeds the deadline, the queue wakes at the deadline and ends delivery (`expired` or `horizon_exceeded`) without another request. Unrepresentable arithmetic ends with `retry_overflow`. Local schedule, cancellation, expiry, and horizon decisions do not spend attempts. FCM transient retries use exponential backoff starting at one minute, plus stable per-delivery jitter, and respect longer provider delays. See [FCM error codes](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/error-codes).
